Dr. Alexander Jacques
Meteorological Product Scientist
New York State Weather Risk Communication Center
University at Albany, SUNY

Hello there! Thank you for visiting this web page. This page provides some information about myself, my current professional efforts, and some additional resource links that I utilize for current and prior career initiatives.

Current Efforts

In July 2025, I joined the New York State Weather Risk Communication Center (NYSWRCC) as their Meteorological Product Scientist. In this role, I design and deploy many of the weather-centric products that the NYSWRCC creates to communicate critical real-time weather information to New York state and local agency stakeholders. Working with other technical team members, many of these products are accessible to stakeholders through several NYSWRCC website tools.

My primary responsibilities involve crafting and operating automated procedures to accomplish several objectives:

1. Aggregate real-time observation and forecast datasets from multiple federal and state agencies, including many resources from NOAA.

2. Post-process the acquired data into efficient formats for access and storage.

3. Statistically evaluate weather data against other non-meteorological datasets critical to NYSWRCC stakeholders.

4. Generate web products, data access tools, and alerting mechanisms for NYSWRCC’s internal forecast team and external New York state and local agencies.

Beyond these core responsibilities, I also have teaching and mentorship initiatives. I assist with the development and teaching of a weather interpretation course NYSWRCC provides to New York state agency personnel throughout the year. The center also supports student interns, where my role supervises and guides undergraduate students who are interested in meteorological research projects.

Prior Efforts

Prior to joining the NYSWRCC, I spent 2011-2025 at the University of Utah working on a variety of different projects and initiatives. I completed my PhD in Atmospheric Science at the university in 2016, where my research focused on analyzing mesoscale pressure perturbations measured by sensors installed on the EarthScope USArray Transportable Array. In addition to this research, I also have prior research in mountain meteorology, air quality, and fire weather disciplines. This included the development and maintenance of real-time data ingest, archival, display, and analysis of air quality measurements deployed on mobile platforms in Utah’s Salt Lake Valley.

I also was involved in several operational instrumentation and technology initiatives. From 2013-2018 I served as a primary hardware and software technician for the University of Utah Mesonet (UUNET) network of weather stations around the Great Salt Lake, and continued maintaining the software from 2018-2025. I also served as a primary developer for the MesoWest program, and a contractor for the company developed out of MesoWest called Synoptic Data PBC.
